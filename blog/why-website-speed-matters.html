Why Website Speed Matters (And How I Make WordPress Fly)

Shaheer Shaikh Jun 15, 20267 min readWordPress

Speed is invisible when it is good and painful when it is bad. A slow website quietly loses visitors, lowers your Google rankings and wastes the money you spend on ads. Studies from Google have consistently shown that the chance of a visitor leaving rises sharply as load time grows from one second to several.

Why speed affects your business

  • Conversions: visitors who wait are less likely to call, buy or fill in a form.
  • SEO: Core Web Vitals are part of Google's page experience signals.
  • Ad costs: landing page experience influences ad quality and cost.
  • Trust: a fast site feels professional; a slow one feels neglected.

My WordPress speed process

1. Start with good hosting

Cheap shared hosting is the most common bottleneck. A quality host with server-level caching and a nearby data centre makes everything else easier.

2. Use a lightweight theme

Heavy multipurpose themes load code for features you never use. I build on lean themes and only add what the design needs.

3. Optimise every image

Images are usually the largest files on a page. I resize them to the size they are displayed, convert them to WebP and lazy-load anything below the fold.

4. Cut the plugins

Every plugin adds code. I audit them, remove anything unused and replace heavy plugins with lighter alternatives.

5. Cache and minify

Page caching, browser caching and minified CSS and JavaScript reduce the work the browser and server do on every visit.

6. Use a CDN

A content delivery network serves your files from a location close to each visitor, which matters a lot for international audiences.

7. Tame third-party scripts

Chat widgets, trackers and embedded videos can slow a page dramatically. I delay or load them only when needed.

The target I aim for on client sites: the main content visible in under two seconds on a typical mobile connection.

How to check your own site

Run your homepage through Google PageSpeed Insights and look at the mobile score and Core Web Vitals. If the numbers are red, your visitors are feeling it too — and it is very fixable.
