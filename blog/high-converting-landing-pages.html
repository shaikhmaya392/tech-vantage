7 Elements Every High-Converting Landing Page Needs

Shaheer Shaikh Jul 15, 20267 min readFunnels

A landing page is not a mini website. It has one job: get the visitor to take one specific action — book a call, request a quote, buy, or sign up. Every element either helps that action or distracts from it. These are the seven I include on every page I build.

1. A headline that states the outcome

The visitor should know within three seconds what you offer and why it matters to them. "Get a free solar quote in 60 seconds" beats "Welcome to our company". Lead with the result, not your name.

2. One clear call to action

Pick one action and repeat it. Multiple competing buttons — call us, read our blog, follow us, download a brochure — split attention and lower conversions. The button text should say exactly what happens: "Get my free quote", not "Submit".

3. Visuals that show the real thing

Real photos of your team, your work or your product build more trust than stock images of people in suits shaking hands. For software, show the actual screen. For services, show before-and-after results.

4. Social proof near the decision

Reviews, star ratings, client logos and short testimonials reduce risk. Place them right next to the form or button, where the visitor is deciding — not buried at the bottom.

5. Benefits, not just features

Features describe what something is. Benefits describe what it does for the customer. "Missed-call text-back" is a feature. "Never lose a lead because you were on a job" is the benefit. Write for the benefit, then back it up with the feature.

6. A short, friendly form

Every extra field costs conversions. Ask only for what you need to take the next step — often name, phone and one qualifying question. You can collect the rest later in your CRM.

7. Speed and mobile layout

If the page takes more than a few seconds to load, many visitors leave before they see your offer. Compress images, avoid heavy scripts, and test the page on a real phone. The button should be easy to tap with a thumb.

Remove the navigation menu from paid-traffic landing pages. Every exit link is an invitation to leave.

Bonus: connect it to follow-up

A landing page is only half of a funnel. The moment someone submits the form, they should get an instant confirmation by SMS or email and a clear next step, like a booking link. That is where I connect the page to GoHighLevel automation — so the conversion keeps working after the click.
