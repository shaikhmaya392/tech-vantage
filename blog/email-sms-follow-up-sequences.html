Email and SMS Follow-Up: The Money Is in the Sequence

Shaheer Shaikh Jun 12, 20267 min readAutomation

Most leads do not buy on the first contact. They are busy, comparing options or simply not ready. Yet many businesses send one follow-up — or none — and then move on. The businesses that win are the ones that follow up consistently and helpfully.

Why sequences work

A sequence is a planned series of messages sent automatically over days or weeks. It keeps you in front of the lead without anyone on your team having to remember. And because it stops the moment the lead replies or books, it never feels like spam to people who have already engaged.

Mix SMS and email

  • SMS is read quickly — use it for short, time-sensitive messages and booking reminders.
  • Email gives room for detail — case studies, guides, pricing and explanations.
  • WhatsApp works brilliantly where customers prefer it, especially in Pakistan and the Gulf.

A proven new-lead sequence

  1. Instantly: SMS — "Thanks for reaching out! Here's a link to pick a time that suits you."
  2. 5 minutes: email with what to expect and a short introduction.
  3. Day 1: SMS check-in if no booking yet.
  4. Day 3: email with a client story or before-and-after.
  5. Day 5: SMS answering a common objection.
  6. Day 10: email with a simple offer or deadline.
  7. Day 30: "Should I close your file?" — a surprisingly effective last message.

How to avoid being annoying

  • Stop the sequence as soon as they reply or book.
  • Make every message useful on its own.
  • Write like a person, not a brochure.
  • Always include an easy way to opt out.
  • Send SMS during sensible hours in the lead's time zone.
The fortune is in the follow-up — but only if the follow-up is worth reading.

Don't forget old leads

Most businesses have hundreds of old enquiries sitting in a spreadsheet. A short reactivation campaign — a friendly "are you still looking for help with…?" — regularly brings back bookings from leads everyone had forgotten.
